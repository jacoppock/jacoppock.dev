## The problem

Hospitals sign hundreds of payer contracts, each with its own rate tables, carve-outs, escalators and fine print. Knowing what a given claim *should* have paid means reading and modeling all of it. When that work is manual, underpayments slip through and rate negotiations rest on guesswork.

## My role

I've shaped ContractIQ from its earliest design through to production, and I lead the ContractIQ and Pricing & Scoring teams. Pricing & Scoring applies ML to denied and underpaid claims to surface the ones most worth pursuing on appeal. Across both, I set technical direction and build the team. <span class="tk">[TK: team size, as approved]</span>

## What it helps us and our clients do

- **Find money owed.** Compare what was paid against what the contract says should have been paid. <span class="tk">[TK: approved impact range]</span>
- **Answer rate questions in minutes, not days.** Model "what if this rate changes?" across a portfolio of contracts.
- **Scale expertise.** Contract knowledge that lived in a few analysts' heads becomes something the whole organization and every client can use.

## What I learned

Our first users were a small team of contract experts, most of them not technical. The hard part wasn't the model. It was representing dense contract language in a way people could read, trust and change themselves. We started simple, aiming for adoption with a lightweight prototype. We quickly learned the problem needed something much bigger and bolder, and rebuilt around that. <span class="tk">[TK: anything else, once approved]</span>
